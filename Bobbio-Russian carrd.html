Behind the Design

Born in Caracas to an Italian family, his identity was shaped by the convergence of two cultural legacies.Educated at La Salle, he developed a strong foundation in discipline, integrity, and perseverance.Driven by a sense of responsibility and a desire to continue the legacy of his family’s construction business, he pursued Civil Engineering at the Universidad Católica in Caracas, grounding himself in the logic of structure, the precision of systems, and the craft of building.

As Venezuela entered a period of profound instability, he was compelled to leave — transforming displacement into evolution. Travel across cultures expanded his perspective and reshaped his creative sensibility, ultimately leading him to establish himself in Madrid.He deepened his expertise in spatial thinking through studies in Interior Design at the IED Istituto Europeo di Design, an institution recognized for its emphasis on material exploration, spatial experience, and sustainable design methodologies in the heart of one of Europe’s most dynamic creative cities.His work reflects both inherited foundations and continuous exploration: a practice where structure meets sensitivity, and where design becomes a bridge between legacy and future possibility.

OAYA: The Roots of Urban ResilienceInspired by the vibrant density of Caracas, OAYA emerges as a beacon of civic well-being within the Petare district. Named after the indigenous Venezuelan word for "Earth," the project reclaims vulnerable structures to transform them into a generative urban fabric. This is architecture as a shared pulse: a space where proximity and collective care redefine the quality of life.In OAYA, citizens become allies in a living laboratory. By integrating Urban Farming and Collaborative Design, the project fosters a symbiotic ecosystem that strengthens community bonds. It is the materialization of a productive sanctuary—one that honors local identity while deploying global innovation to restore the balance of the urban landscape.Developed under the guidance of the Istituto Europeo di Design (IED):

The Architecture of Sustenance

Every project starts with a digital twin, allowing ideas to be tested and improved before construction begins. This process helps create more efficient and sustainable solutions for each location.By combining bioclimatic design, hydroponic and aeroponic systems, advanced materials, and innovative technologies, architecture becomes part of the natural environment instead of working against it.

Guided by bioclimatic strategies and the Urban Ponics catalog, these elements are not mere components; they are the threads of a new urban fabric. The mission is to transform the modern city into a productive, resilient sanctuary—a place where the cold precision of technology and the soft pulse of nature coexist in a state of perfect, enduring harmony.
